# Architectural investment ROI

1. **Exact proof layers and Production-shaped fixtures:** catch SQL, Date, sequencing and scale classes; broad leverage with moderate harness cost.
2. **Bound scoring critical path:** prevents whole-stack scoring outages; substantial but contained backend change with a high integrity-test burden.
3. **Navigation/session and intent-state separation:** fixes repeated native disruption; focused reliability scope with a strong physical-test burden.
4. **Semantic dependencies and current jobs:** remove multiple R3 and financial lifecycle traps; design-heavy but avoid accumulating exceptions.
5. **Director receipts, GO and recovery:** remove the largest routine owner burden; depend on backend contracts.
6. **Observability, resource admission and capacity:** detect degradation and make causal diagnosis possible; foundational and should begin first despite the ROI list order.
7. **Rehearsed fallback and restore:** low complexity relative to completion protection; physical cards remain valuable.
8. **Caching, pooling and measured scaling:** useful once query costs are understood; more compute alone cannot fix unbounded algorithms.
9. **Durable evidence and impact orchestration:** prevent confidence drift and repeated manual certification.
10. **Infrastructure replacement:** conditional only; high migration and operational cost, with unproven benefit before an optimized Supabase benchmark.

The first engineering hour should remove knowledge and control gaps and repeatable critical failures, rather than polish the product or migrate providers. Relative complexity and risks are in the backlog; actual costs and hours are unknown.
